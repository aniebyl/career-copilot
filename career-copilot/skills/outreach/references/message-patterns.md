# Outreach message patterns

All under ~120 words. Hook → why them → one small ask → easy out. In the user's voice.

## Warm referral ask (you know them)

> Hi [Name] — [genuine personal opener tied to your real history]. I'm exploring [role type],
> and saw [Company] is hiring a [role]. Given your seat there, would you be open to [a quick
> referral / 15 minutes to hear how you see the team]? Totally fine if not — either way, good
> to reconnect. [Optional: "Happy to send a short blurb you can forward."]

## Intro request (via a mutual)

> Hi [Mutual] — hope you're well. I'm targeting [role] at [Company]; I think [Target] on the
> [team] would be a great person to learn from. Would you be comfortable making a quick intro?
> Happy to send a 2-line forwardable note to make it easy.

## Cold-but-relevant (no tie — use sparingly)

> Hi [Name] — I've been following [specific thing their team shipped]. I'm a [your one-line
> identity] and [the single most relevant proof point]. If you're open to it, I'd value 15
> minutes on [specific topic]. No worries if the timing's off.

## Principles

- The **easy out** ("no worries if not") raises reply rates — keep it.
- Reference something **real and specific**; generic notes get ignored.
- A **forwardable blurb** makes referrals frictionless — offer one.
- Never overstate closeness; if it's cold, own it briefly and lead with relevance.
